\title{Hybrid Cost Shaping for Multi-UAV Task Allocation under Delayed Sensing and Perishable Tasks}

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous multi-UAV task allocation is commonly solved by bipartite matching when the team observes the full task set and costs reduce to travel versus capability.
Under \emph{Windowed Pop-up Strike} (WPS) conditions---hard time windows, local and delayed sensing, burst arrivals, and attrition---visibility-masked myopic matching leaves a large gap to an omniscient oracle.
We study \emph{hybrid} allocators that retain classical assignment as the combinatorial engine while a learned module reshapes its inputs (task priorities and a soft reserve fraction).
On the primary suite WPS\_hard (\(N{=}100\) paired seeds), Att-RAH improves the evaluation score \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) relative to Local-Hungarian: the paired bootstrap 95\% confidence interval (CI) for the mean difference excludes zero.
Matched MLP and urgency ablations, and follow-on suites that alter the decision interface (timed commits; coalition escort), do not support an additional claim that set attention outperforms non-attention controls under the same hybrid engine and training budgets.
All results are simulation-only; Global-Hungarian is reported solely as an information upper bound.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Multi-UAV systems, task allocation, Hungarian algorithm, hybrid learning, partial observability, time windows
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) is often cast as assignment between agents and tasks~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian,gerkey2004formal,korsah2013comprehensive}.
When sensing is global and objectives are dominated by distance and capability fit, periodic Hungarian matching and market-based methods such as the consensus-based bundle algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba,dias2006market} are strong baselines.
In that regime, replacing the combinatorial core with end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) has not consistently produced gains over classical assignment.

This paper studies a setting in which complete-information myopic matching is a poor inductive bias:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hard windows:} pop-up attack, reconnaissance, and intercept tasks expire; misses are penalized.
  \item \textbf{Local / delayed sensing:} tasks are not globally known; each UAV discovers them within a sensing radius or after a delay.
  \item \textbf{Burst arrivals:} correlated pop-ups punish greedy over-commitment of the fleet.
\end{itemize}
We refer to this family as \textbf{WPS} (Windowed Pop-up Strike).
The fair classical competitor is \textbf{Local-Hungarian}: Hungarian matching on open tasks with edges restricted to each agent's known-task set.
\textbf{Global-Hungarian} ignores sensing limits and is reported only as an information oracle.

\paragraph{Locked claim.}
We show that a hybrid allocator (Att-RAH) that reshapes Local-Hungarian costs with learned priorities improves mean \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) over Local-Hungarian on WPS\_hard under paired bootstrap 95\% CIs; set-attention-specific superiority over matched MLP or urgency controls is \emph{not} established on the evaluated suites.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item WPS scenario family and evaluation scores \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) / \(S_{\mathrm{ESC}}\) emphasizing perishable and escort outcomes.
  \item Hybrid priority-and-reserve modules (MLP-RAH, Att-RAH) coupled to visibility-masked Hungarian matching, with ablations that isolate reserve and learned priority.
  \item Paired-seed evaluation (\(N{=}100\)) with bootstrap CIs on WPS\_hard (primary) and condensed stress / interface suites (WPS\_attn, WPS\_commit, WPS\_escort).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Classical allocation.}
The Hungarian algorithm~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} remains a standard bipartite solver.
Market-based and auction methods, including CBBA~\cite{choi2009cbba}, support distributed multi-robot assignment~\cite{dias2006market}.
Performance-impact (PI) heuristics extend bundle construction by measuring insertion effects on an agent's schedule~\cite{zhao2016pi}.
Taxonomies of MRTA clarify single-task versus multi-task robots and instantaneous versus time-extended assignment~\cite{gerkey2004formal,korsah2013comprehensive}.
Unlike open-information benchmarks, our Local baselines enforce the same visibility mask as the learned hybrids.

\textbf{Learning for task allocation.}
End-to-end policies that select discrete assignments (including attention over task embeddings~\cite{vaswani2017attention}) must solve both scoring and combinatorial feasibility.
When costs are dense and matching structure is available, pure RL often underperforms classical solvers.
Our earlier TBTA experiments used multi-head DQNs over task tokens in a PettingZoo environment~\cite{terry2021pettingzoo}; they motivate keeping Hungarian as the assignment engine rather than replacing it.

\textbf{Hybrids.}
Learning to rank or shape costs for a classical optimizer is a recurring pattern in operations research and robotics.
We adopt that pattern under partial observability and hard deadlines: the network outputs continuous priorities (and optionally commit locks or pair scores), while feasibility and conflict resolution remain classical.

\section{Problem Setting}
\subsection{Agents, tasks, and information}
Let \(\mathcal{A}_t\) be the set of live UAVs and \(\mathcal{T}_t\) the set of open tasks at discrete time \(t\).
Each agent \(a\in\mathcal{A}_t\) maintains a known-task set \(\mathcal{K}_{a,t}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_t\) updated by sensing radius \(R_{\mathrm{sense}}\) and threat-disclosure delay \(d\).
A \emph{local} allocator may assign \(a\) only to tasks in \(\mathcal{K}_{a,t}\).
Heterogeneous types (fighter/recon variants) constrain capability; intercept and escort tasks may require multiple agents.

\subsection{WPS dynamics}
Episodes combine static strike/recon missions with dynamic threats that spawn intercepts and online arrivals.
Configurable parameters include \(R_{\mathrm{sense}}\), \(d\), window length \(W\), burst size, fail rate, and evaluation weights.
Suites used in this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{WPS\_hard} (primary): tight windows, strong delay, bursts.
  \item \textbf{WPS\_easy} / \textbf{WPS\_burst}: softer and harder variants for transfer checks.
  \item \textbf{WPS\_attn}: larger fleet, dual-region bursts, no team knowledge sharing.
  \item \textbf{WPS\_commit}: dual-front setting with timed commits and a rematch penalty.
  \item \textbf{WPS\_escort}: escort coalitions protecting recon UAVs; score \(S_{\mathrm{ESC}}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Baselines}
Local-Cap-Greedy, Local-CBBA-Replan, and Local-Hungarian share the visibility mask.
Global-Hungarian (and Global-Coalition on escort) omit the mask (oracle).
Escort experiments also include Local-PI-Coalition~\cite{zhao2016pi} and Local-CBBA-Coalition under multi-slot residual matching.

\subsection{MLP-RAH and Att-RAH}
Both modules map the current team--task state to per-task priorities \(w_t\in[0,1]\) and a reserve fraction \(\rho\in[0,1]\).
Hungarian edge costs on known pairs use
\begin{equation}
c(a,t)=\mathrm{dist}(a,t)-\alpha\,\mathrm{cap}(a,t)-\beta\,w_t-\gamma\,\mathrm{urgency}(t),
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with unknown edges removed.
The \(\lceil\rho |\mathcal{A}_t|\rceil\) farthest agents may be held idle (soft reserve).

\textbf{MLP-RAH} encodes a fixed-length global feature vector with a multilayer perceptron.
\textbf{Att-RAH} builds agent and task tokens (position, type, urgency, scarcity, remaining demand, idle/capability flags), pads to at most 16 agents and 32 tasks, and applies a 2-layer Transformer encoder~\cite{vaswani2017attention} (\(d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}64\), 4 heads, feed-forward width \(128\), dropout \(0.1\)).
Priorities are read from task tokens; \(\rho\) from a pooled summary.

\textbf{Ablations.}
\emph{Att no-reserve} forces \(\rho{=}0\).
\emph{Att no-priority} replaces the learned \(w_t\) with engineered urgency/scarcity features while retaining the same allocator.

\subsection{Training (RAH)}
Policies are trained on WPS\_hard for 280 episodes (Adam, learning rate \(10^{-3}\)).
The step reward is the change in \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\); checkpoints are selected by validation \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) every 40 episodes (12 eval episodes).
Exploration uses \(\varepsilon\)-annealing from \(0.45\) to \(0.05\).
Inference uses periodic replanning (interval 20) plus event triggers (new threat, agent failure, reset).

\subsection{Interface variants (secondary)}
\textbf{Commit} (WPS\_commit): the module outputs priorities and per-agent commit locks; Local-Hungarian assigns only free agents.
\textbf{Coalition} (WPS\_escort): the module scores agent--task pairs for multi-slot escort tasks; coalition Hungarian consumes soft scores.
Matched MLP and hand-crafted urgency controls share the same interface.
These variants test whether changing the decision interface yields attention-specific gains; they are not part of the primary claim.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
We treat \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) as an \emph{evaluation score} encoding preference for on-time completions over misses, with a small travel regularizer aligned to WPS\_hard weights:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{WPS}}
=
12\,n_{\mathrm{on\_time}}
-
30\,n_{\mathrm{missed}}
-
0.01\,\frac{\mathrm{total\_distance}}{\max\_coord}.
\label{eq:swps}
\end{equation}
On-time rate and missed-window count are co-primary operational metrics.
On WPS\_commit, rematch cost is included in the reported score.
For escort,
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{ESC}}
=
S_{\mathrm{WPS}}
+
20\,n_{\mathrm{prot\_rec}}
-
30\,n_{\mathrm{recon\_loss}}
+
20\,\mathrm{escort\_coverage},
\label{eq:sesc}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{escort\_coverage}\) is the fraction of escort-required steps with a fighter near the protected recon UAV.
Weights are heuristic; we do not claim uniqueness of the weighting.
A legacy completion mass score is recorded only as a secondary diagnostic.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol}
Unless noted, each method--suite pair uses \(N{=}100\) episodes with seeds \(0,\ldots,99\).
For comparisons to Local-Hungarian we report the paired mean difference and a percentile bootstrap 95\% CI (2000 resamples).
Multiple algorithms are reported for context; the primary confirmatory contrast is Att-RAH versus Local-Hungarian on WPS\_hard.
We do not adjust for multiplicity across secondary suites.

\subsection{Primary results (WPS\_hard)}
Table~\ref{tab:hard} summarizes means and CIs for \(\Delta S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) versus Local-Hungarian.
Att-RAH attains mean \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}=-125.5\pm80\) with CI \([+0.7,+26.6]\) for the paired difference, which excludes zero.
MLP-RAH is directionally similar but its CI includes zero (\([-5.7,+21.4]\)).
Cap-Greedy and CBBA-Replan underperform Local-Hungarian under the same mask.
The gap from Att-RAH to Global-Hungarian remains large (\(\approx 45\) points), consistent with information asymmetry rather than solver failure alone.

On WPS\_burst, Att-RAH is comparable to Local-Hungarian.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The primary evidence supports hybrid cost shaping---not set attention as such---on WPS\_hard against Local-Hungarian.
The positive Att-RAH CI is narrow at the lower end (\(+0.7\)), and the no-priority ablation is competitive, so urgency/scarcity features likely explain much of the gain.
On harder asymmetry (WPS\_attn) and alternate interfaces (commit, escort), matched non-attention controls are not outperformed; on escort, the MLP pair scorer surpasses the attention encoder under the evaluated budget.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Results are limited to discrete-time simulation; there is no flight or hardware-in-the-loop validation.
\(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{ESC}}\) use heuristic weights.
Training budgets and architectures are fixed; larger models or longer training might change secondary outcomes but were not explored exhaustively.
Secondary suites are exploratory and not multiplicity-adjusted.

\section{Conclusion}
Under WPS\_hard, Att-RAH improves \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) over visibility-masked Local-Hungarian with a paired bootstrap 95\% CI excluding zero.
Set attention does not, by itself, outperform matched MLP or urgency controls on the stress and interface suites studied here.
The practical implication is to treat classical assignment under local sensing as the core solver, and to evaluate learned modules by whether they improve that fair baseline---not by encoder depth alone.
