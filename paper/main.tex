\begin{abstract}
Aerospace multi-UAV mission management often reduces task allocation to bipartite matching when the team shares a complete common operating picture (COP) and costs are dominated by travel and capability fit.
Under time-critical strike and reconnaissance conditions with \emph{delayed cueing}, \emph{local sensing}, \emph{hard mission windows}, burst arrivals, and attrition---here termed Windowed Pop-up Strike (WPS)---visibility-masked myopic matching leaves a large gap to an omniscient COP oracle.
We study hybrid allocators that retain classical assignment as the combinatorial engine while a learned module reshapes edge costs through task priorities and a soft reserve fraction (MLP-RAH / Att-RAH).
On the primary suite WPS\_hard (\(N{=}100\) paired seeds), Att-RAH attains a higher mean evaluation score \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) than Local-Hungarian (mean \(\Delta\approx +8.7\)), but the paired bootstrap 95\% confidence interval (CI) includes zero (CI \([-8.1,+26.0]\)).
Local-Hungarian remains competitive with learned hybrids under the evaluated training budget, while Cap-Greedy and CBBA-Replan underperform it under the same visibility mask.
Matched non-attention controls and interface extensions (timed commits; coalition escort) do not support an additional claim that set attention outperforms MLP or urgency baselines under the same hybrid engine.
Results are simulation-only; Global-Hungarian is an information upper bound, not an operational competitor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multi-UAV cooperative missions for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance (ISR), and time-critical strike require repeated assignment of heterogeneous air vehicles to spatially distributed tasks~\cite{poudel2022survey,shima2006multiple}.
When sensing is global and objectives are myopic travel-versus-capability costs, the Hungarian algorithm~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} and market-based methods such as the consensus-based bundle algorithm (CBBA)~\cite{choi2009cbba,dias2006market} are strong baselines.
Trajectory-level mixed-integer formulations further couple routing and timing under idealized information~\cite{richards2002aircraft}.
In that regime, replacing the combinatorial core with end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) has not consistently outperformed classical assignment.

Aerospace command-and-control (C2) settings often violate those assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hard mission windows:} pop-up attack, reconnaissance, and intercept tasks expire; misses degrade mission value.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete COP:} tasks are disclosed through local sensing radius and cueing delay rather than instantaneous team broadcast.
  \item \textbf{Burst arrivals and attrition:} correlated pop-ups and vehicle loss punish greedy over-commitment.
\end{itemize}
We refer to this family as \textbf{WPS} (Windowed Pop-up Strike).
The fair classical competitor is \textbf{Local-Hungarian}: Hungarian matching restricted to each UAV's known-task set (incomplete COP).
\textbf{Global-Hungarian} ignores sensing limits and is reported only as an omniscient-COP oracle.

\paragraph{Contribution statement.}
We evaluate hybrid allocators that reshape Local-Hungarian costs under incomplete COP on WPS\_hard: Att-RAH yields a positive mean \(\Delta S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) versus Local-Hungarian whose paired bootstrap 95\% CI includes zero under the evaluated budget; set-attention-specific superiority over matched MLP or urgency controls is \emph{not} established.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An aerospace-oriented WPS scenario family and evaluation scores \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\)/\(S_{\mathrm{ESC}}\) emphasizing perishable and escort outcomes under incomplete COP.
  \item Hybrid priority-and-reserve modules (MLP-RAH, Att-RAH) coupled to visibility-masked Hungarian matching, with ablations isolating reserve and learned priority.
  \item Paired-seed evaluation (\(N{=}100\)) with bootstrap CIs on WPS\_hard (primary), showing that Local-Hungarian remains a strong fair baseline against hybrids under the evaluated budget, with stress and interface extensions in the appendices.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{MRTA taxonomies and classical solvers.}
Gerkey and Matari{\'c}~\cite{gerkey2004formal} and Korsah \emph{et al.}~\cite{korsah2013comprehensive} organize multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) by single- versus multi-task robots and instantaneous versus time-extended assignment.
The Hungarian algorithm~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} remains a standard bipartite engine.
Unlike open-information benchmarks, our Local baselines enforce the same visibility mask as the learned hybrids.

\textbf{Market-based and UAV mission assignment.}
Market methods, including CBBA~\cite{choi2009cbba}, support distributed multi-UAV assignment~\cite{dias2006market,poudel2022survey}.
Extensions address time-sensitive replanning~\cite{chen2021cbba} and schedule-impact scoring (performance impact, PI)~\cite{zhao2016pi}.
Genetic and optimization-based UAV assignment formulations target cooperating uninhabited vehicles under coupling constraints~\cite{shima2006multiple}.
Recent aerospace work studies communication-aware and learning-augmented swarm allocation~\cite{xiong2026cata}.
We retain CBBA/PI as Local baselines but evaluate them under the same incomplete-COP mask as Hungarian hybrids.

\textbf{Planning under timing and information constraints.}
Mixed-integer trajectory planning illustrates the cost of enforcing collision and timing constraints when models are centralized~\cite{richards2002aircraft}.
Our focus is discrete assignment under delayed disclosure rather than continuous trajectory optimization.

\textbf{Learning and hybrids.}
End-to-end policies that select discrete assignments, including attention over tokens~\cite{vaswani2017attention}, must jointly solve scoring and combinatorial feasibility.
When matching structure is available, hybrids that learn scores for a classical solver are a recurring pattern.
We adopt that pattern under partial observability: networks output priorities (or locks/pair scores), while conflict resolution remains classical.
Prior TBTA experiments in a PettingZoo environment~\cite{terry2021pettingzoo} motivate keeping Hungarian as the assignment engine.

\section{System Model and WPS Scenarios}
\subsection{Agents, tasks, and information}
Let \(\mathcal{A}_t\) be the set of live UAVs and \(\mathcal{T}_t\) the set of open tasks at discrete time \(t\).
Each agent \(a\in\mathcal{A}_t\) maintains a known-task set \(\mathcal{K}_{a,t}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_t\) updated by sensing radius \(R_{\mathrm{sense}}\) and threat-disclosure delay \(d\).
A \emph{local} allocator may assign \(a\) only to tasks in \(\mathcal{K}_{a,t}\) (incomplete COP).
Heterogeneous fighter/recon types constrain capability; intercept and escort tasks may require multiple agents.

\subsection{Mission dynamics}
Episodes combine static strike/recon missions with dynamic threats that spawn intercepts and online arrivals.
Configurable parameters include \(R_{\mathrm{sense}}\), \(d\), window length \(W\), burst size, fail rate, and evaluation weights.
Table~\ref{tab:suites} summarizes locked suites.
WPS\_hard is the primary confirmatory suite; WPS\_attn stresses multi-front asymmetry; WPS\_commit and WPS\_escort are interface extensions (Appendix~\ref{app:ext}).

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Baselines}
Local-Cap-Greedy, Local-CBBA-Replan~\cite{choi2009cbba,chen2021cbba}, and Local-Hungarian share the visibility mask and comparable replan triggers.
Global-Hungarian omits the mask (oracle).
Escort experiments additionally use Local-PI-Coalition~\cite{zhao2016pi} and Local-CBBA-Coalition under multi-slot residual matching (Appendix~\ref{app:ext}).

\subsection{MLP-RAH and Att-RAH}
Both modules map the current team--task state to per-task priorities \(w\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal{T}_t|}\) and a reserve fraction \(\rho\in[0,1]\).
Hungarian edge costs on known pairs \((a,t)\) with \(t\in\mathcal{K}_{a,t}\) use
\begin{equation}
c(a,t)=\mathrm{dist}(a,t)-\alpha\,\mathrm{cap}(a,t)-\beta\,w_t-\gamma\,\mathrm{urgency}(t),
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with unknown edges removed.
Up to \(\lceil\rho |\mathcal{A}_t|\rceil\) farthest agents may be held idle (soft reserve).
Default blend coefficients follow the implementation defaults (\(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) fixed across compared hybrids).

\textbf{MLP-RAH} encodes a fixed-length global feature vector with a two-hidden-layer MLP (width 128).
\textbf{Att-RAH} builds agent and task tokens (position, type, urgency, scarcity, remaining demand, idle/capability flags), pads to at most 16 agents and 32 tasks, and applies a 2-layer Transformer encoder~\cite{vaswani2017attention} (\(d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}64\), 4 heads, feed-forward width \(128\), dropout \(0.1\)).
Priorities are read from task tokens; \(\rho\) from a pooled summary.

\textbf{Ablations.}
\emph{Att no-reserve} forces \(\rho{=}0\).
\emph{Att no-priority} replaces learned \(w_t\) with engineered urgency/scarcity while retaining the same allocator.

\subsection{Training}
Policies are trained on WPS\_hard for 280 episodes (Adam, learning rate \(10^{-3}\)).
The step reward is \(\Delta S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\).
The replay buffer stores transitions of predicted value, \(\rho\), and priorities; updates minimize mean-squared temporal-difference and supervised regression losses (batch size 64, discount \(\gamma{=}0.95\), target network sync every 40 updates; buffer cap \(4{\times}10^{4}\)).
Checkpoints are selected by validation \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) every 40 episodes (12 eval episodes).
Exploration uses \(\varepsilon\)-annealing from \(0.45\) to \(0.05\).
Inference uses periodic replanning (interval 20) plus event triggers (new threat, agent failure, reset).
Further details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
We treat \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) as an \emph{evaluation score} encoding preference for on-time completions over misses, with a small travel regularizer aligned to WPS\_hard weights:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{WPS}}
=
12\,n_{\mathrm{on\_time}}
-
30\,n_{\mathrm{missed}}
-
0.01\,\frac{\mathrm{total\_distance}}{\max\_coord}.
\label{eq:swps}
\end{equation}
On-time rate and missed-window count are co-primary operational metrics.
For escort missions,
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{ESC}}
=
S_{\mathrm{WPS}}
+
20\,n_{\mathrm{prot\_rec}}
-
30\,n_{\mathrm{recon\_loss}}
+
20\,\mathrm{escort\_coverage},
\label{eq:sesc}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathrm{escort\_coverage}\) is the fraction of escort-required steps with a fighter near the protected recon UAV.
Weights are heuristic; sensitivity under alternate weightings is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:sens}.
A legacy completion-mass score is recorded only as a secondary diagnostic.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol}
Unless noted, each method--suite pair uses \(N{=}100\) episodes with seeds \(0,\ldots,99\).
For comparisons to Local-Hungarian we report the paired mean difference and a percentile bootstrap 95\% CI (2000 resamples).
The primary confirmatory contrast is Att-RAH versus Local-Hungarian on WPS\_hard.
Secondary suites are exploratory and not multiplicity-adjusted.

\subsection{Primary results (WPS\_hard)}
Table~\ref{tab:hard} summarizes means and CIs for \(\Delta S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) versus Local-Hungarian.
Att-RAH attains mean \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}=-194.1\pm99\); the paired mean difference versus Local-Hungarian is \(\widehat{\Delta}\approx +8.7\) with CI \([-8.1,+26.0]\), which includes zero.
Att-RAH-no-reserve has the best hybrid mean (\(-189.5\pm96\)) but likewise a CI including zero.
MLP-RAH does not improve on Local-Hungarian (CI \([-20,+11]\)).
Cap-Greedy and CBBA-Replan underperform Local-Hungarian under the same mask.
The gap from Att-RAH to Global-Hungarian remains large (\(\approx 63\) points), consistent with information asymmetry rather than solver failure alone.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
On WPS\_hard, hybrid cost shaping yields a directional but non-significant mean improvement of Att-RAH over Local-Hungarian under paired bootstrap CIs.
Local-Hungarian remains a strong fair baseline: Cap-Greedy and CBBA-Replan are substantially worse under the same mask, and learned hybrids do not reliably exceed it under the evaluated budget.
The large remaining gap to Global-Hungarian points to incomplete COP---not classical matching failure---as the dominant bottleneck.
On harder asymmetry (WPS\_attn) and alternate interfaces (Appendix~\ref{app:ext}), matched non-attention controls are not outperformed; on escort, an MLP pair scorer surpasses the attention encoder under the evaluated budget.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Results are limited to discrete-time simulation without flight or hardware-in-the-loop validation.
\(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{ESC}}\) use heuristic weights; Appendix~\ref{app:sens} shows that Att-RAH versus Local-Hungarian CIs still include zero under miss-weight \(\pm 20\%\) and a zero distance term.
Training budgets and architectures are fixed (Att-RAH checkpoint selected by validation \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) within a 280-episode budget).
Secondary suites are exploratory and not multiplicity-adjusted.
Communication channel impairments are not modeled beyond knowledge masking.

\section{Conclusion}
Under WPS\_hard, Att-RAH does not significantly improve \(S_{\mathrm{WPS}}\) over visibility-masked Local-Hungarian under paired bootstrap 95\% CIs (mean \(\Delta\approx +8.7\), CI includes zero).
Set attention does not, by itself, outperform matched MLP or urgency controls on the stress and interface suites studied here.
For aerospace mission management, the practical implication is to retain classical assignment under incomplete COP as the core solver, and to treat learned cost shaping as an optional module that must demonstrate paired gains against that fair baseline.
